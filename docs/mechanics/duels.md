# Battle lobbies

A **duel** is two players fighting each other because they both chose to. Nothing
in the world stages one: a lobby is opened by a player and lives entirely on
invitations, so it is private to the people standing in it.

## Opening one

Anyone may open a lobby, and the host decides on the way in whether they are
**fighting** or only **watching**. A host who stages the fight for other people
is a spectator in their own lobby.

From there the host calls people in, by friend code, from their friends list or
from a trainer's profile, and says what each is called in as: somebody to fight,
or somebody to watch. Nobody has to be a friend to be called. Anybody else in the
lobby may call people in too, but only to watch, since the second seat is the
host's to arrange. An invitation can be declined, and a blocked player cannot be
called at all.

Two people fight. Everybody else in the lobby is watching.

## Getting ready

Each fighter brings up to **six** pokemon of their own, or fewer where the host
has set a smaller team, chosen the way a raid party is chosen. The party is
theirs to change until the fight starts, and changing it takes back their
**ready**.

Both fighters have to be ready before the host may start. That is the difference
between a duel and a raid: a raid lobby stands open in the world and the host
starts it when they like, while a duel has a second person in it who also chose
to be there.

A pokemon cannot be brought if it is already fighting, waiting in another lobby,
still an egg, put away, or barred by the rules below. A party of nothing but
fainted pokemon cannot be readied.

## The rules of the fight

While the lobby is still gathering, the host may set what the fight allows:

| Rule                        | What it does                                                    |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Moves**                   | How many moves each pokemon may use, from four up to eight      |
| **Abilities**               | How many abilities each may bring, from one up to four          |
| **Held items**              | How many held items each may bring, from one up to eight        |
| **Team size**               | How many pokemon a side may field, from one up to six           |
| **Highest base stat total** | No cap, or a cap from 400 up to 680                             |
| **Bans**                    | No legendaries, no mythicals, or no form a held item puts on    |

A lobby starts on the mainline's own shape: four moves, one ability, one held
item, a full six, and nothing banned.

Each limit is a ceiling rather than an allowance. A pokemon fights with what it
actually has, cut down to the limit. What comes first on its sheet is what it
keeps, so the order of its moves, abilities and held items matters.

Changing a rule takes both sides' **ready** away, since the fight they agreed to
was under the old rules. A party longer than the new team size loses its tail,
and a pokemon the new rules bar is taken out of it.

## The fight

An ordinary trainer battle: no boss on either side, so a knockout that takes the
last pokemon on both sides at once is a **draw**.

Everyone in the lobby watches it, fighters and spectators alike, and everybody
sees the same fight.

**A duel costs nothing and pays nothing.** No gold changes hands, no pokemon is
handed over, and neither party carries the fight out with it: health, statuses
and eaten berries are all left where they were. It is a fight for its own sake,
which is what lets two friends run one as often as they like.

The one player-versus-player fight that does settle is a
[gym seat](battle-opponents.md#gym-seats), where the challenger's party carries the wear
out and gold moves.

## Leaving

Anybody may leave a lobby, and a lobby the host leaves closes. A fighter who
walks out takes their party with them and the other side simply waits for
somebody else to be called in.
