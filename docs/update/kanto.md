# Kanto

Kanto is the first release of Overwander. It is a world to walk, the original
151 pokemon living in it, and everything you do with them.

Every major release is named after a region. This one is named for the region
its pokemon come from.

## The world

The world is generated from one seed as you walk it.

- **25 kinds of country**, from coral reef and savanna to volcano and glacier.
- No loading screens, and no edge to be turned back from.
- Two players in the same field at the same time see the same things.
- The pokemon, the berries, the raids and the people at the crossroads all turn
  over on their own schedules.

## The sky

Twenty-six kinds of weather drift across the world. They are not rolled per
chunk, so walking out of the rain takes a walk.

- A sky **favours a type or two**. Those types turn up twice as often and come
  out with a floor under every stat.
- **Four skies favour every type at once.** A **meteor shower** doubles shiny
  odds, a **fata morgana** doubles hidden ability odds, a **dark day** meets
  shadows, and a **fogbow** hands over an egg move.
- A **dark day** is the one sky you cannot see across. The board goes black
  except for a pool of light around you and around each landmark.
- Weather stands in the world rather than on the glass, so the whole storm
  sweeps when you turn the camera.

## Pokemon

All 151, and each one is settled before you walk up to it.

- Its level, nature, gender, ability, size and moves are fixed. There are no
  rerolls.
- **Shinies**, **hidden abilities**, runts and record-breakers.
- **Shadow** pokemon, which are harder to raise until you put them right.
- A **featured family** every so often, the same one for the whole world. Far
  more of them appear, their shiny odds are better, and they pay more candy.

## Catching

There are no wild battles. You walk up to a pokemon with whatever is in your
bag.

- Throw a ball, feed a berry to settle it, or back away.
- A failed throw can make it bolt for good.
- **Fourteen kinds of ball**, each good somewhere different: the first throw,
  the tenth throw, in the dark, in the water.
- A meeting opens on the ball you last threw, unless you turn that off.

## What walks beside you

The pokemon at your side changes what you find. Every ability it uses out here
is one it already had.

- **Arena Trap**, **Illuminate** and **No Guard** draw three more pokemon out.
  **Stench** keeps two away.
- **Keen Eye** and **Intimidate** keep the weak ones away. **Hustle**,
  **Pressure** and **Vital Spirit** draw the strong ones out.
- **Compound Eyes** finds what a pokemon is carrying. **Frisk** tells you before
  you have thrown anything.
- **Synchronize** passes on its nature. **Cute Charm** draws out the opposite
  gender. **Flame Body** halves an egg's walk. **Pickup** finds items as you go.

## Battles

Both sides fight at once, in real time. Nobody waits for a turn.

- A move winds up, swings and recovers. A heavy move leaves you open while it
  gathers.
- A quick pokemon lands several hits before a slow one connects.
- Your pokemon pick their own moves, and never spend one on a target it cannot
  touch.
- **Switching is a walk across the field**, so a move aimed at the pokemon
  leaving lands on whoever took its place.
- Conditions and weather carry out of the fight, so a party is worth looking
  after.

## Raids

A lair holds a legendary for a few hours, and anybody may join.

- Bring up to six pokemon. Up to twenty players can join, and you can watch
  instead of fighting.
- The boss hits your whole party at once. It stands still at the start while you
  set up.
- Beating it closes the lair until it restocks. Everyone in the lobby is paid
  the same.
- **Mythicals** appear only when somebody spends a relic to call one out.
- You can bring a friend into a lobby, to fight or to watch.
- **Team Rocket grunts** block a path with a shadowed party and pay out when you
  beat them.

## The league, and other trainers

The road is full of people who want a fight.

- **Duelling trainers** stand at their own cells. Each is an Ace or an expert in
  one type, and each pays a purse.
- **Gym leaders** hold the region's eight badges, one badge each.
- The **Elite Four** want every badge before they will see you. The **Champion**
  wants all four of their marks.
- **Gym seats** are held by other players. Beat the party standing there to take
  a share of their gold and sit on the cell yourself.
- **Duels** are two players who both chose to fight, in a private lobby, with
  whoever they invite watching. Nothing is staked and nothing is lost.

## Quests, badges and titles

- A **quest board** asks for things you were going to do anyway and pays for
  them: catches, hatches, walks, raids, trades.
- **Three dailies and a weekly hunt** run on top of it, the same for everybody
  in the world. They count from the moment the day first sees you.
- **Achievements** climb four tiers on lifetime counters, per deed, per type,
  and per class of trainer beaten.
- You wear one **title** over your name, such as League Challenger or Master
  Collector.

## Raising

Nothing levels up by turning up to a fight. You raise it.

- **Candy** is shared across a family, so catching Caterpies raises your
  Butterfree.
- You choose which stats a pokemon trains. **Wings** raise that ceiling.
- Walking together makes a pokemon fond of you, and some evolve on nothing else.
- Pokemon evolve by level, stone, friendship or trade. An **Everstone** stops
  all of it.

## Eggs

An egg is a pokemon you have not met yet. What is inside is decided the moment
you pick it up.

- Find eggs in **nests**, or ask the **breeder** for one.
- They hatch on steps, from a few hundred to several thousand.
- A pokemon with **Flame Body** at your side halves the walk.
- What comes out inherits from its parents, so a good pair is worth keeping.

## People

Someone different stands at the crossroads every few hours, and you cannot tell
who until you get there.

- The **vendor** sells balls and medicine.
- The **breeder** makes an egg from two of your pokemon.
- The **daycare lady** takes half the remaining walk off an egg.
- **Nurse Joy** patches up the party.
- The **groomer** makes a pokemon fond of you far faster.
- The **Move Reminder** gives back a move it forgot.
- The **Fossil Maniac** sells fossils. The **Fossil Scientist** revives them,
  which is the only way to meet an Omanyte, a Kabuto or an Aerodactyl.
- A **Team Rocket grunt**, who is not here to help.

Each helps you once. After that it is a walk to another crossroads.

## Items

Hardly anything is for sale. You dig it up.

- **Item caches** hold whole stashes, and **berry patches** fruit all day.
- Odd things happening in the open leave gems, plates and wings behind.
- A buddy with **Pickup** finds things while you walk.
- Gear keeps working in a fight: Leftovers, plates, weather rocks, and a
  **Utility Belt** that lets a pokemon carry two things.
- Berries fire once, exactly when needed. No medicine works once a fight starts.

## Trading

A pokemon changes hands in two ways.

- **The auction house** is open to everybody. A lot leaves your bag when you
  list it. A bid leaves your purse at once and comes back the moment somebody
  outbids you. One lot each per day, and only the genuinely scarce may be
  listed.
- **A trade with a friend** swaps your pokemon for theirs, with gold either way.
  What you offer is held while they think about it, and a traded pokemon can
  evolve the way only a traded one can.

## Friends and gifts

- Add a friend by their **friend code**, so somebody has to hand it to you
  first.
- Requests can be taken back. Blocking ends the whole thing quietly.
- **Gifts** are set aside for you: a pokemon, a stack for your bag, or a meeting
  where you still throw the ball.
- **Four gifts** are waiting on day one: Bulbasaur, Charmander, Squirtle, and
  twenty Poke Balls. You can take all three starters.

## Getting about

- **Click** where you want to go, or **walk on the keyboard**. The arrows and
  WASD move a cell at a time, and holding a direction keeps walking.
- Walking into somebody turns you to face them. Enter acts on whatever you are
  facing. The directions, Interact and Menu can all be rebound.
- **Turn the camera** with a right-drag, or a two-finger twist on a phone. Cards
  open on a hold where there is nothing to hover with.
- Signed in twice, you still have one walk. The screen that is not walking
  stands down and hands its steps over.

## Where the art comes from

Every pokemon on screen is animated from **SpriteCollab**, the Pokemon Mystery
Dungeon sprite collection. Its artists are credited per sprite in that project's
own files. The overworld people are the **Pokengine** community's fan sheets.

Everything in the interface is **pokesprite**: the item icons in the bag, the
eighteen type badges, and the three marks a move's category is shown by.

[Credits](../credits.md) names all of it, sheet by sheet, with the terms each
one comes under.

## A few promises

- Nothing goes off when you walk over it. You act on what you meant to act on.
- Everyone sees the same world at the same moment.
- Morning, evening and night are read off your clock, and they change what you
  meet.
- Nothing timed can be rushed by changing your device.
- A battle plays back exactly as it happened.

## Since launch

- A fight names and draws whoever you actually fought, in the history and in the
  replay.
- A shiny throws its glint as it walks into a fight.
- The board draws less and draws it faster, on phones especially.
- A shadow is the shape of the thing that cast it, laid the way the light falls.
- Notices open the panel they are about.
