# Friends

A player can ask another player to be their friend. Until the other answers, the
request stands where both of them can see it: the asker can take it back, and the
person asked can accept or decline.

If two players ask each other at the same time, the second request finds the
first and the friendship is made on the spot.

A player may have up to **100 friends**.

## Friend codes

Every trainer has a **friend code**: twelve digits in three groups, minted the
first time they look at it and theirs for good. "Add a friend" takes a code and
nothing else.

A code rather than a name, because two players may call themselves the same
thing and a player who renames themselves would become unfindable. A code rather
than an email address, because an address is something a player has for other
reasons. Nobody can be found without their code, and handing it over is the
whole check on who may ask.

Nothing is found for a code nobody plays under, for a banned account, or for the
reader's own.

## Blocking

Blocking somebody undoes the friendship, drops any request in either direction,
and stops either of them asking again.

A block is **one-sided to read**: nothing tells the blocked player, which is the
point of one. Lifting a block does not restore the friendship it undid.

## Trading

A trade is a swap between two friends, and the only place a pokemon passes from
one player to another without gold deciding it.

The proposer names the pokemon they are offering, what they want back, and
whatever gold rides along:

- **A named pokemon.** They ask for one of the receiver's in particular.
- **An open ask.** They ask for something, and the receiver picks it.
- **Gold either way.** Gold sent with the offer, or gold asked of the receiver.

**The offered pokemon leaves the proposer as the offer is made**, exactly as an
auction lot does, so an offer is something the receiver can trust while they
think about it. Gold offered goes with it. What the receiver is giving is checked
again when they accept, since it may have gone into a raid or an auction while
the offer stood.

One offer per direction at a time. Either side may end it: the proposer takes it
back, the receiver declines, and the pokemon and the gold go home either way.

A traded pokemon arrives with its **friendship reset** and counts as having
changed hands, which is what opens a [trade evolution](raising.md#evolution).
Neither side may trade a pokemon that is fighting, waiting in a lobby, still an
egg, locked, or walking beside them as a buddy.
