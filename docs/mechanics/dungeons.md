# Dungeons

A dungeon is a landmark with floors inside it. It stands out in the country or
in the caves, never in a town, and it is walked room by room.

There are three kinds.

| Kind          | Where                                             | Floors      | At the end                                |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Hideout**   | Anywhere a syndicate works                        | 3 to 5, down | The syndicate's boss                      |
| **Dungeon**   | Only where the biome hosts a legendary's lair     | 3 to 7      | A legendary of the biome that cannot flee |
| **Frontier**  | Where a Battle Frontier house stands              | 7, up       | The house's Brain                         |

A hideout or a dungeon turns up in about one chunk in twelve. A Frontier tower
is the Battle Frontier house that was already standing there.

## A run

A dungeon is cleared **once per window** (3 hours), like a raid. Losing is not
the end of the window: the next attempt starts again from the first floor.

- **The party is locked in at the entrance.** It is the party for the whole run.
- **Damage stays.** Health, status and fainting carry from fight to fight.
- **Only your own medicine heals.** Potions, Revives and the like work as usual.
  Nurse Joy and a level from candy do nothing for a pokemon locked into a live
  run.
- **Losing a fight ends the run.** The party is freed and the run goes back to
  the entrance.
- **A stash is taken once per window.** A restart does not pay it again.
- **Giving up the run** frees the party. Climbing out by the ladder does not: the
  run keeps where you were, and the party stays locked in until the window ends.

A fainted pokemon stays out of the next fight. A party with nobody standing
cannot fight, so heal them or walk out.

## Floors

Going in takes you off the world map and onto the dungeon's first floor, which
you walk on the board the way you walk the caves. A floor is a grid of rooms, 3
by 3 on the first floors and up to 5 by 5 deeper down, each room 5 cells across
with doorways between them. You arrive on a ladder up; the stairs down are
somewhere else on the floor.

- **Trainers watch a line.** Each stands facing a doorway and sees 4 cells ahead,
  until a wall or anything standing stops the look. Step into that line and they
  come to fight you. Beaten, they stay where they are and watch nobody.
- **A wild horde** in a Dungeon stands in its room. Walk up to it and press to
  fight it; beaten, it is gone.
- **Items lie on the floor** as Poké Balls. Face one and press to pick it up: a
  stash, the floor's key, or its pass.
- **The stairs** ask for one of two things: **the guard beside them beaten**, or
  **the floor's pass picked up**. Stepping onto them takes you down.
- **The ladder up** takes you back to the floor above, and out of the dungeon from
  the first floor. The run keeps where you were.

A Frontier floor is one room: you arrive at one end, the trainer stands in front
of the stairs, and they see you the moment you come in.

### Layout

Every hideout and dungeon floor has one layout rule. A hideout leans towards
spinner tiles, warp pads, locked doors and barriers. A dungeon leans towards ice,
cracked floors, obstacles and ledges. Either can roll any of them.

| Layout            | What it does                                                                              |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Spinner tiles** | A lane of arrows runs through a room. Step on it and it carries you along, through the doorway at its end. |
| **Warp pads**     | A pad sends you to its pair. Islands of rooms no door joins are only reached by pad.       |
| **Ice floor**     | A step keeps sliding until rough ground, a doorway or a wall stops it.                    |
| **Cracked floor** | A cracked doorway holds once and breaks behind you. Step into the hole and you fall to the floor above, or out of the dungeon from the first floor. |
| **Obstacles**     | A boulder, a rock, a tree or water fills a doorway. Strength pushes a boulder a cell (and a boulder pushed into a hole fills it), Rock Smash breaks a rock, Cut fells a tree and Surf crosses water. A party member has to know the move. The stairs never need one; what is behind one is a stash. |
| **Locked doors**  | A door opens with the floor's key, found on the same floor.                               |
| **Barriers**      | Two sets of barriers, one open at a time. Pressing a switch flips which.                  |
| **Ledges**        | A doorway you hop down one way only. A wrong drop can cost rooms, never the stairs.       |

And how much of it you can see:

| Light        | What is shown                                                                         |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Lit**      | The whole floor                                                                        |
| **Unmarked** | The whole floor, but not who or what is in a room until you have stood in it           |
| **Dark**     | Pitch black past 2 cells, the way a cave is. A party member with Illuminate or Flash, or holding an Explorer Kit, sees 5 |

Every floor can be finished without any move or light: the generator walks it cell
by cell to check.

## Who you fight

| Kind         | In the rooms                                   | On the stairs               | At the end                              |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Hideout**  | A grunt with three shadows, one of each band   | An executive with six rares | The boss: five rares and a local legendary, all shadows |
| **Dungeon**  | A wild horde of 1 to 6 of the biome's uncommon and rare pokemon | A horde of 6 | A legendary of the biome's lairs |
| **Frontier** | A trainer with three from the Factory's crate  | The same                    | The Brain                               |

A dungeon's hordes start at level 25 to 35 on the first floor and climb 10 a
floor. Hordes are fought, not caught.

Every Frontier floor plays under **the Brain's own house rule**, so the climb is
practice for the fight at the top. Under the Factory's rule every floor is
fought with three rented on the spot.

## What clearing pays

| Kind         | Clearing pays                                                                                   |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hideout**  | The boss' mark, 120,000 to 250,000 gold, a stash off the Champion's table, and one of the boss' six, shadowed, at level 10 |
| **Dungeon**  | The legendary, met at level 50 without fleeing, and 80,000 gold                                 |
| **Frontier** | The house's silver symbol, or gold once silver is held, and 200,000 to 400,000 gold             |

The syndicate's boss is met nowhere else: a Team Rocket cell holds a grunt or an
executive, never the boss.
